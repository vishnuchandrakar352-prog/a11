The Cornell Note-Taking Method

Developed at Cornell University in the 1950s, this simple page layout turns ordinary notes into a ready-made study tool.

1. Set up the page

Divide each page into three areas before class starts:

  • A narrow cue column on the left, about a third of the width.
  • A wide notes column on the right.
  • A summary strip across the bottom, five or six lines tall.

2. During class: the notes column

Write in the right-hand column only. Use short phrases, abbreviations and simple sketches rather than full sentences. Skip a line between ideas so there is room to add details later. Your aim is to capture the main ideas, not every word.

3. After class: write cues

Within a day, reread your notes and write questions or key words in the left column next to each idea. A cue such as "What are the three stages?" turns a block of notes into something you can test yourself on.

4. Summarise in your own words

At the bottom of each page, write two or three sentences that sum up the whole page. Summarising forces you to decide what matters, and it gives you a fast way to review before an exam.

5. Study with the cues

Cover the notes column, read each cue and answer it out loud. Then check. This is self-testing, and it is far more effective than simply rereading. Mark any cues you missed and come back to them first next time.
