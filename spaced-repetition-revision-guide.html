Spaced Repetition for Revision

We forget most new information within days unless we review it. Spacing those reviews out is one of the most reliable ways to make learning last.

1. The forgetting curve

In the 1880s the psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus showed that memory fades quickly after learning, then more slowly. Each time you review, the curve flattens, so you can wait longer before the next review.

2. Growing intervals

A simple starting schedule:

  • Review after 1 day.
  • Then after 3 days, then 7.
  • Then after 14 and 30 days if there is time.

3. Test, do not just reread

Each review works best as a quiz: flashcards, Cornell cues or blank-page recall where you write everything you remember. Struggling a little to remember is what strengthens the memory.

4. Mix your topics

Instead of studying one topic for hours, mix several in one session. This is called interleaving. It feels harder, but it helps you tell similar ideas apart, which is exactly what exams ask you to do.

5. Plan backwards from the exam

Put the exam on a calendar, then fit reviews in before it. Start early so the longer gaps fit. Our revision planner does this for you: enter the date you learned a topic and your exam, and it lays out the reviews.
